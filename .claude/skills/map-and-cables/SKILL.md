# Map and cable work

The map is hand-built: coastline rings in `SEA_LANDS`/`LANDS`, city coordinates in `CITIES`,
sea routes precomputed into `CABLE_PTS` by `scripts/build-cable-paths.py`, then decorated at draw
time by `bowCables()`.

## The layer that is actually wrong is rarely the one that looks wrong

This cost three failed attempts. Always measure the raw data before blaming the renderer.

| Layer | Measured over land | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `CABLE_PTS` raw waypoints | **0.9%** (42/4675) | the sea router's own floor. Accept it |
| after fan + lane + corridor offsets | **27.3%** (1480/5414) | the renderer was the bug |
| after the guards + `clearLand()` | **1.5%** (82/5414) | the rest are genuine land links |

The remaining 1.5% is **correct**: Jambi–Palembang, Palangkaraya–Banjarmasin,
Samarinda–Balikpapan, Solo–Semarang, and three Waeve rail hops are supposed to run over land.
Do not chase 0%. `tests/prd-cablebow.test.js` locks the threshold at 2%.

## Measure four things, never one

A single metric can always be gamed. Each of these has been "improved" by making another worse:

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Land points | < 2% | cables must not cross Borneo |
| Detour | ~2.2x | `CORRIDOR=80` won on overlap and produced 15.8x detour |
| Sharp turns > 45° | ~570/5414 | a 3-point polyline with a lateral offset becomes a spike |
| Overlapping pairs | < 80 | the original complaint |

`CORRIDOR=80` looked like a win on overlap alone and was the worst result of the session.

## Offsets need a land guard, and the guard needs a real `onLand`

A 16 px sideways shove is invisible in open sea and puts a cable on the beach in a 10 km strait.
Every offset path must check `onLand()` and shrink toward the original waypoint until it is back in
water. Watch for early returns that skip the check — the `if(!w) return ...` path was unguarded and
carried most of the points.

`clearLand()` runs **after** `ALLPOLY` is rebuilt for the current region, and pulls each land point
to the nearest water point **on its own route**. Averaging with immediate neighbours barely helps,
because when a whole run sits over one island the average is also over that island.

## Projection

`project:(lon,lat)=>[(lon-90)/66*W,(28-lat)/40*H]` for SEA and IDN. Latitude spans 28 to −12
(40°) so Timor and Roti are not clipped. An earlier 26..−9 window cut Dili off the bottom edge.
Do not narrow it back.

## Coastline rings

`SEA_LANDS` holds 158 rings over 22 landmasses from Natural Earth. Islands are assigned to a
landmass by centroid when the ring area is ≥ 1.0, and by nearest vertex below that — using the
nearest vertex everywhere made one ring absorb the whole of Sumatra.

`tests/prd-rings.test.js` asserts every SEA city sits inside its own island's ring. It caught
`Sorong: land=papw but ring=pap`, `Dili: land=tls but ring=ntt`, and Madura/Kangean labelled as
Bali.

## Colour

`cableCol()` still returns only three colours (`#14A88C`, `#2F7BEA`, `#A05AE0`), so every cheap
cable is the same green and 99 pairs read as one thread. The user asked for 8–10 stable colours
derived from a hash of `cid`, still grouped by price. Not done yet.

## What "sejajar" means

The user's latest request is that cable angles look aligned, not sprawling. That cannot be solved
with another render offset — the routes follow the coastline. It needs the routes themselves
simplified or their headings quantised (e.g. to 15° or 45° multiples), then offset parallel per
segment like traffic lanes. `CABLE_PTS` is generated by `scripts/build-cable-paths.py`, so the
change belongs there, and the regeneration must be re-measured against all four metrics above.
